Plan-ahead: pork marinates 24–48 hr and roasts ~6–8 hr. Start the day before.
Same-day: bread is a ~4–4.5 hr same-day bake at 70°F. The finishing sauce is the pan reduction — the marinade + drippings reduced down, no separate build.

Part 1  Cuban Bread AP flour, NOT bread flour — tender, pressable

IngredientBaker %Weight
AP flour (~10.5–11.5% protein)100%750 g
Water (warm; ~100g reserved to bloom)63%475 g
Lard (rendered pork lard — structural)4%30 g
Sugar2%15 g
Salt2%15 g
ADY (bloomed)1.5%11 g
  1. Bloom ADY: yeast + sugar in ~100g water at 100–110°F, 5–10 min to foam. Over ~115°F kills it.
  2. Mix: flour, salt, lard, bloom, remaining water. Knead to moderate gluten — soft, NOT windowpane. ~8–10 min.
  3. Bulk: ~1.5–2 hr to doubled at 70°F — by the dough, not the clock.
  4. Shape: 8 × 160g, short ~8″ blunt-ended torpedoes, seam down.
  5. Proof: 45–60 min until puffy.
  6. Score one lengthwise slash. Bake 400°F w/ steam first 10 min, then dry; ~20–22 min to pale golden (200–205°F internal). Brush hot with lard/butter for a soft, pressable crust.

Temp Targets this stage — the bread

Bloom water100–110°F — foam in 5–10 min. Over ~115°F kills the yeast.
RiseBulk & proof at 70°F room — by the dough, not the clock.
BakeOven 400°F, pull at 200–205°F internal — pale golden, NOT dark.

Part 2 · The Pork

Marinated Pork

Part 2  Marinade No salt — salt the meat (see cook)

Badia Mojo Marinade (base — garlic + onion already in it)1,170 g
Olive oil or lard (held — floats in a bath)120 g
Ground cumin (fresh)20 g
Mexican oregano dried10 g
Onion sliced thin1 large
Cracked black pepper12 g
Bay leaves5
Maggi seasoning (umami, rec.)56 g
Salt (NOT in marinade — salt the meat)—

Bloom Non-negotiable — fat carries aromatics into the meat

  1. Warm oil/lard to 250–300°F — shimmering, not smoking (below olive oil's ~400°F smoke point).
  2. Add cumin, stir 30–45 sec to nutty/fragrant — burns fast, kill heat when aromatic.
  3. Off heat: oregano, pepper, bay. Stir residual heat 15–30 sec. Cool 5 min. (no fresh garlic — Badia is already garlic-heavy)

Combine & Marinate

  1. Non-reactive container (never aluminum): Badia + bloomed mix + onion + Maggi. Whisk hard; it separates into fat/water phases, normal. (no salt — the marinade becomes the reduction; salt the meat directly instead)
  2. Butt into a 4qt Cambro, pour over to submerge (~1.5L covers a 6 lb butt in this vessel). Fridge 24–48 hr; one flip at 12 hr for the fat-line surface.
  3. 6 lb: 24 hr sufficient, 48 hr max. Don't exceed — surface goes mealy.
Why bloom is mandatory: fat drives cumin/oregano warmth into the meat. Skip it and aromatics sit on the surface and wash off.

Temp Targets this stage — bloom & marinate

Bloom fat250–300°F — shimmering, not smoking. Cumin scorches in seconds; kill heat the moment it's fragrant.
MarinateFridge 38–40°F, 24–48 hr (24 sufficient, 48 max — past that the surface goes mealy).

Part 3 · Roast & Reduction

Cook & Pan Reduction

Part 3a  Cook Stainless pan only — never nonstick with acid at temp

Oven, fat cap up, rack over a stainless pan275°F
Salt the butt directly before roasting (marinade has none)—
Marinade goes IN the pan, butt on the rack above — drippings fall in, onion cooks down~1.5L
Top with water if the pan reduces low / threatens to scorch—
Pull at internal195°F
Rest, tented45–60 min
Total estimate (stall-dependent)~6–8 hr
Foil by bark, not clock: roast open until the bark is dry, deeply colored, firm — doesn't smear (~few hours). THEN foil to protect it through the stall and finish. Tent the edges specifically if they run ahead. Burnt edges → bitter bits in the drippings, which become the sauce — protect them.

Part 3b  Pan Reduction The marinade reduced — this IS the finishing sauce

No separate mojo build — the bloomed aromatics already rode the marinade into the pan and cooked down with the drippings. Pour pan liquid + drippings into a pot and finish:

  1. Hard rolling boil first — mandatory, the marinade was raw-pork-contaminated. Not a gentle simmer.
  2. Reduce to coats-the-spoon (nappe) — but keep enough volume to hold the sliced pork. Make extra; reduce less far if you're running short. Taste for salt; cut with water/unsalted stock if it sharpens.
  3. No finishing citrus — the reduced Badia carries plenty of acid. Optional: a squeeze of fresh lime off-heat only if it tastes flat/dull (brightness, not more sour).
  4. Hold the sliced pork in the warm sauce — let it drink it up before building. This is the step that makes it next-level; make enough sauce to do it.
Body & tuning: if it won't reach nappe before salting out, thicken with a cornstarch slurry (gentle simmer, don't hard-boil after — acid breaks it). Too acidic → pinch sugar or ~0.6g baking soda at a time. Fat stays in — it rounds the acid and carries flavor.

Temp Targets this stage — roast & reduce

RoastOven 275°F, fat cap up. Foil by bark-set, not clock.
PullPork internal 195°F, then rest tented 45–60 min.
ReduceHard rolling boil first (safety), then reduce to nappe — keep volume to soak the slices.

Why 275–300°F, not 400°+: the press is a dual-target — bread crisps fast, cheese melts slow. Too hot = scorched crust, cold cheese. Low and patient lets heat reach the center before the bread burns. IR-gun the surface — a flat-top has zones; find the even one and re-check between sandwiches as it drifts.

One-lard system: the same rendered lard goes in the bread dough, brushes the crust, and fries the pork aromatics — one ingredient ties the sandwich together.

Key Principles hard-won — the "why"

Bread: AP, not high-glutenTender crumb that compresses in the press. High-gluten bakes tough and rubbery. Pale, thin crust — don't bake dark.
Flood the pan WITH the built marinadeReverses old advice: the v1 failure was flooding with pure-acid marinade. This marinade has a bloomed savory base — in the pan it catches drippings and reduces into the sauce. Top with water if it scorches.
One aromatic build, at the sourceThe bloomed cumin/oregano in the marinade carries through to the reduction — no second mojo build needed. The marinade onion cooks down in the pan for umami.
Reduced Badia carries the acidNo finishing citrus needed — the base is heavily acidic and concentrates. Optional squeeze only if the sauce tastes flat/dull, for brightness not sour.
Stainless, never nonstickAt roasting temps/durations with acid, nonstick is out.
