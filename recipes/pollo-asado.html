Break up the achiote brick in the citrus first — whisk it into the orange + lime juice before adding oil, or it stays gritty and dyes-streaky. Cilantro goes in at the finish only — cooked in the marinade it turns muddy and flat; raw at the end it stays bright. Marinade window is 2 hr minimum, 24 hr max — past 24 hr the acid breaks down the meat to mush.

Method · Sear · Troubleshoot

Cook & Finish

Method Active ~15 min — serve immediately

  1. Cube: cut thighs into 1–1.5″ (2.5–4 cm) pieces, uniform for even cooking.
  2. Mix: whisk the marinade smooth — break up the achiote in the citrus first — until the color is uniform.
  3. Marinate: coat every piece, refrigerate 2–24 hr (overnight is the sweet spot). Never past 24 hr.
  4. Temper: pull the chicken 20 min before cooking. Heat griddle / cast iron until screaming — IR 450–500°F.
  5. Sear: single layer, do not crowd, 2–3 min/side for char. Let the pan recover between batches — crowding steams, no char.
  6. Chop: all chicken back on the surface, chop with two spatulas, let it sit 30–60 sec, toss. Builds crispy edges.
  7. Finish: fresh lime squeeze (both profiles); fresh cilantro on the green only. Pull at 165–175°F, serve now.
Chop-and-sear is the same move as shawarma — cubing then chopping on the hot surface multiplies the seared edge without overcooking the interior, so you get crispy edges and juicy centers. The achiote turns bitter if the paste is old or scorched: use fresh paste and medium-high, not nuclear, once the chicken is down.

Notes Scaling · serving

Scaling: the per-450 g ratios scale linearly — 1.5 kg is 3.3× a single pound. Serving: tacos, burritos, rice bowls; pairs with pickled onion, crema, and salsa. Same family as the shawarma — oil-carried marinade, hot iron, chop finish.
