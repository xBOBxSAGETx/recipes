The blend method: make the bone broth in advance (a weekend project), store it in the fridge or freezer, then transform it into finished phở any weeknight in 30–45 min — long extraction separated from quick assembly. Dilution principle: extract hard, season to ratio, dilute at service. A concentrated base diluted down keeps full flavor and body; an under-extracted broth is missing compounds you can’t add back.

Stage 0 · Make Ahead · 24 Hours

Bone Broth Base

The base is everything. Use marrow bones (flavor) + knuckle bones (gelatin). The ratio is 1:1 bones to final volume — add 30% extra water to cover evaporation over the long simmer. This is the weekend project; the weeknight assembly (page 3) is fast because this is done.

Bone Roasting reserve the rendered fat — it’s your tallow for the spice bloom

  1. Straight from frozen: bones go from freezer to oven — no thaw, no parboil.
  2. Roast hot: 425–450°F for ~1 hour.
  3. Color target: golden-mahogany with dark spots — dark spots are fine, black is not.
  4. Save the fat: reserve the rendered tallow for blooming the spices at assembly.

Color & Char Cues judged by eye — over-charring turns it bitter

Bone colorGolden-mahogany. Dark spots OK, not black.
Aromatics char30–40% black spots. Not full char — full char causes bitterness.

24-Hour Simmer gentle bubbles, never a rolling boil — that’s what keeps it clear

  1. Set it: roasted bones + water (the 30% extra for evaporation).
  2. Hold the heat: simmer at ~94°C / 200°F — gentle bubbles, no rolling boil (a hard boil emulsifies fat and turns it murky).
  3. Finish to number: top up to the target volume at the end, strain. Target: 7.0 Brix.
  4. Store: fridge up to 1 week, or freezer for months.
Body comes from gelatin, not luck. If the chilled broth doesn’t set to a wobble, you’re short on knuckle bones or the simmer was cut early — the full 24 hr at a gentle 200°F is what converts collagen to the gelatin that gives phở its weight on the tongue.

Method bloom in the reserved tallow off-heat — the broth quenches it

  1. Char aromatics: high broil — onion halves, shallots, ginger (skin on), garlic. Flip for even char. Target 30–40% black spots, not full char (bitter).
  2. Bloom spices: beef tallow in the pot, get it hot, kill the heat. Add the spices ~30 sec until fragrant, then the charred aromatics — they sizzle in the residual heat.
  3. Add broth + pre-season: pour in the 3 L bone broth (it quenches everything), bring to a gentle simmer. Add salt (35 g), rock sugar (55 g), hạt nêm (20 g); stir to dissolve. Taste — 95% there, slightly salty.
  4. Infuse: without brisket 30–45 min; with brisket ~3 hr to probe-tender. Maintain the volume — big loss means the heat’s too high.
  5. Final adjustment: strain out the aromatics + spices. Add fish sauce (8–15 ml) until you catch a slight fishiness; optional 250 ml oxtail concentrate. Target 8.7 Brix — over, dilute at service.
  6. Serve: noodles in the bowl, ladle hot broth, add sliced brisket or raw steak (it cooks in the broth). Sides: bean sprouts, Thai basil, lime, hoisin, sriracha.
