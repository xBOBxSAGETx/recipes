Part 1  Marinade No salt — salt the meat (see cook)

Badia Mojo Marinade (base — garlic + onion already in it)1,170 g
Olive oil or lard (held — floats in a bath)120 g
Ground cumin (fresh — scale down if mild)20 g
Dried Mexican oregano10 g
Onion sliced thin1 large
Cracked black pepper12 g
Bay leaves5
Maggi seasoning (umami — optional, rec.)56 g
Salt (NOT in marinade — salt the meat)—

Bloom Non-negotiable — fat carries aromatics into the meat

  1. Warm oil/lard over medium-low — not smoking.
  2. Add ground cumin. Stir 30–45 sec until nutty and fragrant. It burns fast — kill heat the second it's aromatic.
  3. Off heat, add oregano, pepper, bay. Stir into residual heat 15–30 sec. (no fresh garlic — Badia is already garlic-heavy)
  4. Cool 5 min to warm, not hot.

Combine & Marinate

  1. Non-reactive container (glass, stainless, food-safe plastic — never aluminum): combine Badia, bloomed fat-aromatic mix, onion, Maggi. Whisk hard — it separates into fat/water phases, normal.
  2. Butt into a 4qt Cambro, pour over to submerge (~1.5L covers a 6 lb butt in this vessel). Fridge 24–48 hr; one flip at 12 hr for the fat-line surface.
  3. 6 lb: 24 hr sufficient, 48 hr max. Don't exceed — surface protein denatures and goes mealy.
Why bloom is mandatory: fat is the carrier that drives cumin and oregano warmth into the meat. Skip it and aromatics sit on the surface and wash off.

Roast · Pan Reduction

Cook & Reduction

Part 2  Cook Stainless pan only — never nonstick at these temps with acid

Oven, fat cap up, on a rack over a stainless pan275°F
Salt the butt directly before roasting (marinade has none)—
Marinade goes IN the pan, butt on the rack above — drippings fall in, onion cooks down~1.5L
Top with water if the pan reduces low / threatens to scorch—
Pull at internal195°F
Rest, tented, before slicing45–60 min
Total estimate (stall-dependent)~6–8 hr
Foil by bark, not clock: roast open until the bark is dry, deeply colored, firm — doesn't smear (~few hours). THEN foil to protect it through the stall and finish. Tent the edges if they run ahead — burnt edges put bitter bits in the drippings, which become the sauce.

Part 3  Pan Reduction The marinade reduced — this IS the finishing sauce

No separate mojo build — the bloomed aromatics rode the marinade into the pan and cooked down with the drippings. Pour pan liquid + drippings into a pot:

  1. Hard rolling boil first — mandatory, the marinade was raw-pork-contaminated. Not a gentle simmer.
  2. Reduce to coats-the-spoon (nappe), but keep enough volume to hold the sliced pork. Make extra; reduce less far if short. Taste for salt; cut with water/unsalted stock if it sharpens.
  3. No finishing citrus — reduced Badia carries plenty of acid. Optional squeeze of lime off-heat only if flat/dull (brightness, not sour).
  4. Hold the sliced pork in the warm sauce to drink it up before building — the step that makes it next-level. Make enough.
Body & tuning: won't reach nappe before salting out → cornstarch slurry (gentle simmer, don't hard-boil after — acid breaks it). Too acidic → pinch sugar or ~0.6g baking soda. Fat stays in — rounds the acid.

275–300°F, not 400°+: dual-target — bread crisps fast, cheese melts slow. Too hot = scorched crust, cold cheese. IR-gun the surface, find the even zone, re-check as it drifts.
Hold the slices: rest sliced pork in the warm reduction so it drinks the sauce up before building — this is the next-level step. Make enough sauce to actually submerge them.

Key Principles v2 hard-won learnings — the "why"

Flood the pan WITH the built marinadeReverses old advice: v1 failed because the marinade was pure acid. This one has a bloomed savory base — in the pan it catches drippings and reduces into the sauce. Top with water if it scorches.
One aromatic build, at the sourceThe bloomed cumin/oregano in the marinade carries through to the reduction — no second mojo build. The marinade onion cooks down in the pan for umami.
Reduced Badia carries the acidNo finishing citrus — the base is heavily acidic and concentrates. Optional squeeze only if the sauce tastes flat/dull, for brightness not sour.
Fat stays in the sauceBuffers acid, carries flavor, correct for a pressed sandwich.
Stainless, never nonstickAt roasting temps/durations with acidic content, nonstick is out.
