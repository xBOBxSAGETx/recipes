Butcher → Marinade → Freeze build ahead — the freeze + thaw does the marinating

  1. Butcher / dice: trim the shoulder clod and cut ⅜–½″ (1–1.5 cm) cubes across the grain — uniform for even searing, cross-grain for tenderness.
  2. Blend the marinade: everything except the beef into a blender — blend smooth (a splash of water to get it moving if needed). It scales clean and coats evenly for bulk.
  3. Combine: toss the diced beef in the blended marinade until every piece is coated — a large tub / bus tub for a full clod.
  4. Portion + freeze: into quart bags (450 g each), press flat, remove air, label with the date, freeze right away — no separate marinate step.

Method toast · soak · char · fry the chile · mount the verde fresh

  1. Toast the chiles. Fire the Ooni. Toast guajillo flat at the cooler mouth/edge — not the screaming center, it scorches — 30–45 sec/side until it puffs and smells nutty; pull the instant it smells toasty (guajillo turns acrid fast). Toast the árbol + chiltepín briefly; they’re thin/tiny and scorch first. No Ooni? Comal/cast iron over medium (~350–400°F). Have hot water ready.
  2. Soak. Push the toasted chiles under the hot water; soak 20–30 min until completely soft — no firm bits, or they blend gritty. SAVE the soaking water.
  3. Char the aromatics — on the Ooni. Char onion slabs and skin-on garlic near the center, hard — blistered, blackened, soft inside. That live-fire char is the salsa’s signature. Peel the garlic.
  1. Two blends, kept apart. (a) Chile paste: drained chiles + a little soaking water, blend smooth (sieve for dead-smooth). (b) Verde: both cans Embasa + charred onion + peeled garlic, blend smooth.
  2. Fry the chile paste — alone. Lard shimmering (~325–350°F); add the chile paste only (it spits — stand back) and fry, stirring, 5–8 min until it darkens and deepens. Frying the chile by itself is what concentrates the heat + flavor — don’t add the verde yet.
  3. Mount the verde fresh — don’t reduce. Off heat (or barest warm), stir the verde blend into the fried chile. Do not cook it down — keep it loose and bright. No salt, no lime by design; Knorr caldo de pollo to taste (~7 g), thin with soaking water.

Phase 3  Cook from Frozen ripping-hot griddle — sear, don’t steam

  1. Thaw: fridge overnight — it seasons as it thaws, and with no acid there’s no mush risk, so a full thaw is fine. Pat dry going in for a clean sear.
  2. Griddle: cast iron / flat-top ripping hot (IR 450–500°F), light oil. Single layer, don’t crowd — pat surfaces dry going in. Hard sear.
  3. Chop-and-sear: add fresh sliced white onion alongside if you like; chop with two spatulas, let it sit 30–60 sec, toss for crispy edges.
  4. Finish & build: pull at medium, 130–140°F. Double-stacked tortillas, the seared beef, then fresh lime, the Phase 2 salsa, raw onion + cilantro, flaky salt. Brightness and heat both come on at the table.
