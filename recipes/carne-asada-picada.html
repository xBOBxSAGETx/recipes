No acid & no heat in the bag — on purpose. Over the season → freeze → thaw cycle, lime would sit on the beef the whole slow thaw and bully the flavor (and soften lean beef). So it’s seasoned light and beef-forward — garlic, cumin, oregano, Maggi, and onion are quiet complements, not a spice rack (the beef and the sear are the star) — and you bring brightness + heat at the taco: fresh lime, flaky salt, and the Part 3 salsa. ~1% total sodium (salt 0.6% + Maggi’s ~0.4%) — light by design; add at the plate, you can’t subtract. Part 2 · Prep · Freeze · Griddle

Make Ahead & Cook

Part 2  Prep & Freeze build the bags ahead — the marinade works through the freeze + thaw

  1. Dice: trim the shoulder clod and cut ⅜–½″ (1–1.5 cm) cubes across the grain — uniform for even searing, cross-grain for tenderness.
  2. Blend: everything except the beef into a blender — blend smooth (a splash of water to get it moving if needed). No grating; it scales clean and coats evenly for bulk.
  3. Combine: toss the diced beef in the blended marinade until every piece is coated — a large tub / bus tub for a full clod.
  4. Portion + freeze: into quart bags (450 g each), press flat, remove air, label with the date, freeze right away — no separate marinate step. The meat marinates as it freezes and thaws.

Cook from Frozen ripping-hot griddle — sear, don’t steam

  1. Thaw: fridge overnight — it seasons as it thaws, and with no acid there’s no mush risk, so a full thaw is fine. Pat dry going in for a clean sear.
  2. Griddle: cast iron / flat-top ripping hot (IR 450–500°F), light oil. Single layer, don’t crowd — pat surfaces dry going in. Hard sear.
  3. Chop-and-sear: add fresh sliced white onion alongside if you like; chop with two spatulas, let it sit 30–60 sec, toss for crispy edges.
  4. Finish: pull at medium, 130–140°F. This is where the lime lives — squeeze fresh lime over, then cilantro, onion, the Part 3 salsa. Double-stacked tortillas, flaky salt. Brightness and heat both come on at the table.

Part 3  Ingredients dried chiles + a canned verde base — upgraded by frying

Guajillo chiles stemmed, seeded — flavor & body4
Pasilla chiles stemmed, seeded — dark, raisiny2
Chiles de árbol stemmed, seeds in — the heat engine12
Chiltepín chiles tiny wild pods — very hot, sharp flash heat~4 g
White onion thick slabs, to char½
Garlic skin on, to char3 cloves
Embasa salsa verde 7 oz / 200 g each can2 cans
Lard or neutral oil — to fry the salsa13 g
Knorr caldo de pollo optional — only if it tastes flatto taste

Method toast · soak · char · blend · fry · balance

  1. Toast the chiles. Fire the Ooni. Toast guajillo + pasilla flat at the cooler mouth/edge — not the screaming center, they scorch — 30–45 sec/side until they puff, darken, and smell nutty; pull the instant they smell toasty (guajillo turns acrid fast). Toast the árbol + chiltepín briefly; they’re thin/tiny and scorch first. No Ooni? Comal/cast iron over medium (~350–400°F). Have hot water ready.
  2. Soak. Push all toasted chiles under the hot water; soak 15–20 min until fully pliable. SAVE the soaking water — it’s the salsa’s liquid and flavor.
  3. Char the aromatics — on the Ooni. Char onion slabs and skin-on garlic near the center, hard — blistered, blackened, soft inside. That live-fire char is the salsa’s signature; cast iron or an oven can’t match it. Peel the garlic.
  1. Blend. Drained chiles, charred onion, peeled garlic, and both cans of Embasa into a blender. Add a splash of reserved soaking water to get it moving. Blend smooth — start thick, you can always loosen. Aim for a sauce that coats a spoon.
  2. Fry it — don’t skip. Heat the lard until shimmering (~325–350°F). Pour in the salsa (it’ll spit — stand back) and fry, stirring, 5–8 min until it darkens, thickens, and the raw chile edge cooks off. Single biggest upgrade; it also fixes the thin volume of canned base.
  3. Cool, then balance. Off heat, rest 10 min — flavors are muddy while screaming hot and resolve as they settle. No salt, no lime by design. If it tastes flat, stir in a pinch of Knorr caldo de pollo — nothing more.
Heat dial — built hot. 12 árbol + chiltepín is genuinely hot — it still bites once built into the taco (the árbol lingers, the chiltepín flashes) — and it’s the only heat at the table (the marinade is heat-free by design). Push hotter: bloom extra árbol/chiltepín whole in the lard 30–45 sec until the oil reddens, before the salsa goes in. Milder? Drop to 6 árbol / 2 g chiltepín.  •  No salt, no lime — on purpose. Embasa is salt-forward and frying concentrates it; the verde base is already tart. Fresh lime + flaky salt finish the meat at the plate — don’t double up.  •  Texture: keep it spoonable, not paste — it has to cling to the crispy diced picada edges; loosen with soaking water if it tightens.  •  Make-ahead: improves overnight as the fried chile rounds out. No cumin/oregano — the meat carries those.
