Carne Asada Picada

Diced shoulder clod · frozen plain · griddle chop-and-sear · the taco brings the brightness, heat & salt

What You Need the bag is just beef — everything else is cook-day

Beef shoulder clod diced ⅜–½″, across the grainthe star
Beef tallow or neutral oil — to searto coat
Maggi light griddle glaze (umami)a splash
Lime + flaky salt at the tacoto finish
White onion + cilantro raw, chopped — at the tacoto finish
Corn tortillas double-stacked, warm2 per taco

Phase 1  Butcher & Freeze freeze plain — no marinade (no acid-mush; a dry surface sears, not steams)

  1. Dice: trim the clod, cut ⅜–½″ (1–1.5 cm) cubes across the grain — cross-grain for tenderness, uniform for an even sear.
  2. Bag + freeze plain: portion into quart bags (450 g), press flat, remove air, date, freeze. Nothing in the bag — you season at the griddle and the taco.

Phase 3  Sear & Build ripping-hot — sear, don’t steam

  1. Thaw + pat BONE-DRY: fridge overnight, then dry hard — thawed beef weeps, and surface water kills the crust.
  2. Ripping-hot griddle (cast iron / flat-top, IR 450–500°F), beef tallow. Single layer, do NOT crowd — crowding drops the temp and steams the beef gray.
  3. Chop-and-sear: let it crust untouched, then chop/toss with two spatulas and let it re-crust — building 15–20% dark caramelized edges. Hit it with a light splash of Maggi near the end — it glazes; don’t drown it or it steams.
  4. Doneness — no thermometer (a ½″ cube overshoots before a probe reads). Pull at deep-charred edges, the cube firmed but still giving, juicy/pink inside. Pull while juicy — shoulder dice has no time to render, so overcooked = dry and tight.
  5. Build: double-stacked warm tortillas → beef → fresh lime → flaky salt → raw onion + cilantro → the salsa (p.2). Brightness and heat both land at the table.

If It Goes Wrong

Tough / chewyOvercooked, or cut with the grain — pull while juicy; dice across the grain.
Gray, no charCrowded or wet surface — single layer, pat bone-dry, recover to 450–500°F.
Tastes flatNo acid/salt in the bag, by design — that’s the taco’s job: lime + flaky salt + salsa.

Phase 2 · The Salsa

Asada Salsa

Salsa de chile de árbol y tomatillo — grandma’s: boiled tomatillo, toasted árbol, garlic, cilantro. Spicy.
Yield
~2 cups (480 ml)
Active
~25 min
Heat
Spicy
Keeps
4–5 days, fridge

Ingredients five things — tomatillo, árbol, garlic, cilantro, salt

Tomatillos fresh, boiled — ~1 lb, store-bought fine~450 g
Chiles de árbol toasted — the heat; SPICY, ~12–15 ga handful
Garlic boiled with the tomatillos3 cloves
Cilantro fresh, raw — leaves + stemsa handful
Salt Diamond Crystal — to tasteto taste

Method boil · toast · blend · salt — no onion, no fry

  1. Boil. Simmer the tomatillos + garlic cloves in water until the tomatillos turn olive-green and soft (~8–10 min). Scoop out; save a little of the cooking water.
  2. Toast the árbol. Light toast on a comal / the Ooni edge — fragrant and bendy, zero black (old chiles tip bitter fast). Optional: bloom a few in a spoon of beef tallow to wake them.
  3. Blend. Boiled tomatillo + garlic, toasted árbol, a handful of raw cilantro (stems too), and salt — blend smooth, loosen with the cooking water to spoonable. No straining (árbol skin is thin), no frying.
  4. Taste. SPICY is the target — add árbol till it bites; salt to taste. Judge on a built taco, not the spoon (fat + tortilla mute it).

Heat = árbol, the only knob — a big handful of the old chiles now; a pinch of chiltepín later (3–5× hotter). Boiled tomatillo is committed; raw-vs-charred tomatillo + raw-vs-boiled garlic are the next dial-in (ask the aunts).
