No acid in the bag — on purpose. Over this bulk season → freeze → thaw cycle, lime would sit on the beef for the whole slow thaw and bully the meat flavor (and soften lean beef). Instead it’s seasoned light and beef-forward — garlic, cumin, oregano, Maggi, and onion are quiet complements, not a spice rack (the beef and the sear are the star) — and you bring the brightness with fresh lime + salsa at the taco. No acid also means the thaw is worry-free: thaw fully in the fridge, pat dry, sear ripping hot. Dice across the grain and pull at medium — it’s beef, not the pollo, so don’t cook it through. No heat in the bag either — salsas + chiles at the table. ~1% total sodium (salt 0.6% + Maggi’s ~0.4%) — light by design; finish with flaky salt + salsa at the plate (add there, you can’t subtract). Prep · Freeze · Griddle

Make Ahead & Cook

Prep & Freeze build the bags ahead — the marinade works through the freeze + thaw

  1. Dice: trim the shoulder clod and cut ⅜–½″ (1–1.5 cm) cubes across the grain — uniform for even searing, cross-grain for tenderness.
  2. Blend: everything except the beef into a blender — blend smooth (a splash of water to get it moving if needed). No grating; it scales clean and coats evenly for bulk.
  3. Combine: toss the diced beef in the blended marinade until every piece is coated — a large tub / bus tub for a full clod.
  4. Portion + freeze: into quart bags (450 g each), press flat, remove air, label with the date, freeze right away — no separate marinate step. The meat marinates as it freezes and thaws.

Cook from Frozen ripping-hot griddle — sear, don’t steam

  1. Thaw: fridge overnight — it seasons as it thaws, and with no acid there’s no mush risk, so a full thaw is fine. Pat dry going in for a clean sear.
  2. Griddle: cast iron / flat-top ripping hot (IR 450–500°F), light oil. Single layer, don’t crowd — pat surfaces dry going in. Hard sear.
  3. Chop-and-sear: add fresh sliced white onion alongside if you like; chop with two spatulas, let it sit 30–60 sec, toss for crispy edges.
  4. Finish: pull at medium, 130–140°F. This is where the lime lives — squeeze fresh lime over, then cilantro, onion, salsas / chiles. Double-stacked tortillas, flaky salt. Brightness and heat both come on at the table.
