The flour's malted barley supplies the enzyme load. 0.5% ADY targets a ~4.5h total room ferment (~3h bulk + ~1.5h pan proof), dropped from 0.6% because the bench rest kick-starts fermentation and the fridge only slows it — the dough ripens a bit in the cold, so less yeast keeps the cold-hold honest. Start here; if they still over-ferment in the fridge, go to 0.4%. If it drags, the granite's colder than figured — nudge up or extend.

Mix Two batches — incorporation only, then fold

Mix straight, then divide by weight for the stretch-and-fold. Each batch makes 12 bagels.

Mixer = incorporation only. At 60% with 14.2% flour the hook can't reach windowpane — gluten's too strong to fully develop in the machine. Run low just until combined, no dry bits, then stop.

Develop by stretch-and-fold: after the mix, bench rest 20–30 min — it relaxes and hydrates the dough so the first fold actually develops it. Then 2–3 S&F sets ~30 min apart through the first ~1.5h of bulk; the rests relax it, the folds build the network. Windowpane is a post-fold check.

~1,815g dough per batch → 12 bagels @ 150g.

The consistency lever: shaping from a bulk-fermented mass — not pre-divided pieces — is why pro bagels come out even without a scale. A short bulk organizes the gluten across the whole dough so each piece ropes uniformly. This is the structural reason for the fork.

Bulk · Shape · Proof · Hold

  1. Short bulk, ~3 h: mix in the morning, then bench rest 20–30 min before folding. Bulk on the counter to ~50% rise; run 2–3 S&F sets, 30 min apart, through the first ~1.5 h. The bulk's job is to organize the gluten, not the main proof — pull by rise, not clock.
  2. Divide from the bulk to 150g. Gently degas as you pre-round — press the big bubbles out (trapped gas = uneven ropes). Bench rest 10–15 min, then rope by flatten → roll up tight → weld the seam on un-floured counter (the roll expels gas). Small ~1.5″ hole = puffs up, not out.
  3. Pan proof — THIS is the main proof, ~1–2 h: rise the shaped bagels on half-sheets at 70°F to final height. This is the rise that makes the bagel; weight it here, not in the bulk. Float-test when they look ready.
  4. Fridge = mostly hold. Bagels go in proofed; they'll ripen a little in the cold (it slows, doesn't stop) — the lowered yeast keeps that in check. Cold-hold for flavor + scheduling, boil straight from cold (brief temper).

Granite note: cold counter is a heat-sink — it slows the bulk (good, keeps it partial) but unevenly. Bulk in a bowl for even temp if you want; the pan proof runs warmer (pan buffers the counter) which is why it finishes the rise.

Boil & Bake

  1. Float test straight from cold: surfaces in ~10s → go. Sinks → temper at room temp, retest. This gates everything.
  2. Boil bath: per ~7 L water add 40 g honey — no baking soda (the alkali thickens and toughens the skin). Gentle rolling boil, never violent.
  3. Boil 30 sec / side. Shorter boil = thinner, more tender crust. Seed wet, straight out of the water (everything blend).
  4. Bake 450°F — 10 min on the boards, 10 min off (clean 50/50, ~20 min): boiled, seeded bagels go seed-side-down on the wet bagel boards (soak them well first — dry burlap scorches and sticks), steam the first few min. At 10 min flip them off the boards onto a stone or sheet pan for the last 10. No pizza steel — it dumps too much heat and dries the heel; the boards give an even start and a stone/pan finishes gentler. Set + even color; ~205–210°F internal confirms done (thick part of the ring, not the hole).
  5. Rest 20–30 min before cutting. Hot crumb reads false-gummy.

Browning Mechanism Honey-only bath — golden, not mahogany

Honey deposits a sugar film → Maillard fuel for color and flavor. Baking soda is out: the alkali browned faster (pretzel chemistry) but set a thicker, tougher skin that pulls away from the crumb — the same delamination that sheds everything-seasoning when you cut. Without it the crust bakes thinner and more tender, but lighter — golden, not the deep mahogany that needs barley malt. Malt is the real color + flavor fix when it's on hand.
