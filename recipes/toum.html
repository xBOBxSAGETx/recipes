Toum

Lebanese garlic sauce · egg-free emulsion · fluffy white paste that holds stiff peaks
Yield
~500 g

15–20 min

4–6 wk fridge

Ingredients Full batch ~500 g — weigh everything

Ice water stabilizes the emulsion60 g
Kosher salt by weight; helps break down garlic6 g
Toum is an egg-free emulsion — the garlic’s own water plus lemon juice suspend the oil droplets, so the 1 : 4 garlic-to-oil ratio (by weight) is non-negotiable: too much oil too fast and it breaks. Never olive oil — it’s bitter and dyes the toum yellow; neutral oil lets the garlic stay clean and white. The ice water keeps the processor cool (heat breaks emulsions) and fresh, firm garlic carries more water for a stronger set.

Method · Rescue · Scaling

Build the Emulsion

Method ~10–12 min of patient streaming — don’t rush the first oil

  1. Prep: peel the garlic, slice each clove lengthwise and remove the green germ (it’s the source of bitterness). A small food processor (~3-cup) works best.
  2. Paste: garlic + salt into the processor. Pulse, scrape, then run 1–2 min to a smooth paste — this is the foundation.
  3. Start: processor running, drizzle the first ~55 g oil in a hair-thin stream over 2+ min, then add 15 g lemon. This is the make-or-break step — do not rush it.
  4. Build: alternate ~55 g oil (slow) then 15 g ice water or lemon, repeating until both are used. ~10–12 min total. It turns fluffy and white.
  5. Check: it should hold stiff peaks, bright white. Too thick → add ice water; processor warm → pause and let it cool.
  6. Rest: jar it and refrigerate 24 hr minimum — the harshness mellows and the texture firms.
If it breaks (soupy, separated): start a new batch — 2 fresh garlic cloves + a pinch of salt to a paste, then stream the broken toum back in slowly as if it were the oil. Egg-white rescue (1 frothy egg white, broken toum streamed in) also works but drops shelf life to 5–7 days. Most breaks come from oil too fast or a hot processor.
