Asada Salsa

Fried guajillo·pasilla·árbol salsa on a tomatillo-verde base · the only heat at the table
Yield
~2 cups (480 ml)
Active
~40 min
Heat
Hot
Keeps
1 week, fridge

Ingredients dried chiles + a canned verde base — weeknight shortcut, upgraded by frying

Guajillo chiles stemmed, seeded — flavor & body4
Pasilla chiles stemmed, seeded — dark, raisiny2
Chiles de árbol stemmed, seeds in — the heat engine12
Chiltepín chiles tiny wild pods — very hot, sharp flash heat~4 g
White onion thick slabs, to char½
Garlic skin on, to char3 cloves
Embasa salsa verde 7 oz / 200 g each can2 cans
Lard or neutral oil — to fry the salsa13 g
Knorr caldo de pollo optional — only if it tastes flatto taste

Method toast · soak · char · blend · fry · balance

  1. Toast the chiles. Comal or cast iron over medium (surface ~350–400°F), or the mouth of the Ooni. Lay guajillo + pasilla flat; toast 30–45 sec/side until they puff, darken, and smell nutty — pull the instant they smell toasty (guajillo turns acrid fast). Toast the árbol + chiltepín briefly; they’re thin/tiny and scorch first. Have a bowl of hot water ready.
  2. Soak. Push all toasted chiles under the hot water; soak 15–20 min until fully pliable. SAVE the soaking water — it’s the salsa’s liquid and flavor.
  3. Char the aromatics. On the comal/stone, char the onion slabs and skin-on garlic until blackened in spots and soft. Pull; let the garlic cool enough to peel.
  1. Blend. Drained chiles, charred onion, peeled garlic, and both cans of Embasa into a blender. Add a splash of reserved soaking water to get it moving. Blend smooth — start thick, you can always loosen. Aim for a sauce that coats a spoon.
  2. Fry it — don’t skip. Heat the lard until shimmering (~325–350°F). Pour in the salsa (it’ll spit — stand back) and fry, stirring, 5–8 min until it darkens, thickens, and the raw chile edge cooks off. Single biggest upgrade; it also fixes the thin volume of canned base.
  3. Cool, then balance. Off heat, rest 10 min — flavors are muddy while screaming hot and resolve as they settle. No salt, no lime by design. If it tastes flat, stir in a pinch of Knorr caldo de pollo — nothing more.
Heat dial — built hot. 12 árbol + chiltepín is genuinely hot — it still bites once built into the taco (the árbol lingers, the chiltepín flashes) — and it’s the only heat at the table (the picada marinade is heat-free by design). Push hotter: bloom extra árbol/chiltepín whole in the lard 30–45 sec until the oil reddens, before the salsa goes in. Milder? Drop to 6 árbol / 2 g chiltepín.  •  No salt, no lime — on purpose. Embasa is salt-forward and frying concentrates it; the tomatillo base is already tart. Fresh lime + flaky salt finish the meat at the plate — don’t double up.  •  Texture: keep it spoonable, not paste — it has to cling to the crispy diced edges of the picada; loosen with reserved soaking water if it tightens overnight.  •  Make-ahead: improves after a night in the fridge as the fried chile rounds out. Don’t add cumin/oregano — the meat already carries those, you’d muddy it.
