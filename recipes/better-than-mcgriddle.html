Better-Than-McGriddle Sandwich

Maple-pocket griddle cakes · seared sausage · American · folded egg · ~4 sandwiches + a spare cake
Yield
~4 + spare
Cakes
9 @ ~58 g
Key Lever
Maple SUGAR
Griddle
~300°F / 150°C
The whole dish turns on one variable: a maple pocket that survives the griddle. It is solved with maple SUGAR (crystallized), folded in last so it stays as discrete crystals — those crystals melt into pockets of caramelized maple inside the cake. Never liquid syrup in the batter: it bleeds, scorches against the surface, and gums the crumb. Liquid maple appears in exactly one place — the finishing butter, brushed on the outside after the cake is set (Component 3, page 2).

1 · Griddle Cakes Baker's % on flour weight · flour = 100% · ~536 g batter

AP flour — the base; everything else is a % of this100%200 g
Maple sugar — structural syrup pockets; folded in LAST, stays crystalline20%40 g
Granulated sugar — exterior browning10%20 g
Baking powder — lift4%8 g
Baking soda — browning + reacts with the buttermilk0.3%0.6 g
Fine salt (by weight)1.5%3 g
Egg — 1 large; structure25%50 g
Buttermilk — tang + tender crumb (the acid lever)92%185 g
Melted butter — plain melted & cooled, NOT browned15%30 g

Mixing & Griddling 9 cakes · greased 3.5–4″ rings · low-and-slow, covered

  1. Whisk the dry — but HOLD the maple sugar. Combine flour, granulated sugar, baking powder, baking soda, salt. Keep the 40 g maple sugar aside; it goes in dead last.
  2. Whisk the wet: egg + buttermilk + the cooled melted butter.
  3. Combine to just-mixed — lumps are fine. Stop the moment the flour disappears; overmixing builds gluten and gives flat, tough cakes.
  4. Fold the maple sugar in LAST, ≤5 strokes. It must stay suspended as discrete crystals, not dissolve — those undissolved crystals are the syrup pockets.
  5. Rest the batter 15 min. Hydration finishes and the gluten relaxes — this is the chew.
  6. Portion + griddle: a #16 disher (scant ¼ cup, ~58 g) into greased 3.5–4″ rings, ~1 cm deep, on a ~300°F / 150°C surface (IR). Covered ~3 min, flip, ~3 min. Low-and-slow so the maple sugar caramelizes inside without scorching the exterior.
Chew variant: swap 25–30% of the flour for bread flour and bump the buttermilk to ~193 g to offset the higher absorption. More structure, a denser pull — closer to a true griddled-bread bite.

Sausage · Maple Butter · Egg · Cheese · Build

Components & Build

2 · Sausage Your breakfast sausage · 4 patties

Breakfast sausage raw, per patty80 g
— if leaner than ~25% fat90 g
  1. Smash to ~4″ wide so it sears flat and finishes ~3.5″ after shrink — matched to the cake diameter.
  2. Sear hard, both sides, on a ~425°F / 220°C surface (IR) for a full Maillard crust. Melt the American on top off-heat (Cheese, right).

3 · Maple Finishing Butter % on butter weight · the ONLY liquid maple

Butter softened, not melted100%40 g
Real maple syrup25%10 g
Flaky salt to finish——
  1. Beat soft butter + syrup to a compound butter — do not melt it; it must cling.
  2. Brush on the cake exteriors during the final 30 s on the griddle. Outside, post-bake — where liquid maple can't gum the crumb.

4 · Folded Egg 2 eggs per sandwich · pale, soft-set, folded to the cake

  1. Beat 2 eggs with a splash of water + a pinch of salt. Water steams to a tender puff; skip milk — it browns and toughens. The McGriddle egg is a folded sheet, not a round basted egg.
  2. Pour thin into a greased ~4″ ring on a ~275°F / 135°C surface (IR) — the coolest stage. Keep it PALE, no browning.
  3. For the soft-scramble texture, drag the egg a few times as it sets into small curds, then stop and let it knit into a sheet.
  4. While the top is still just-wet, fold into a ~3.5–4″ pad matched to the cake; cover ~20–30 s. Carryover finishes it — pull it tender (overcooked egg turns rubbery and weeps). The diameter match is what sits the stack even.

Cheese 1 slice per sandwich · no build

One slice elite-quality American, melted on the hot sausage off the heat. American's emulsifying salts give the clean, even melt this needs. Do not substitute aged cheese — it breaks and weeps oil.

5 · Assembly Serve immediately — cakes stale fast once the maple sugar recrystallizes

  1. Stack on the bottom cake (maple face up): sausage with its melted cheese, then the folded egg pad.
  2. Cap with the top cake, maple face down. Close and eat hot.

Read & Fix Judge by the crumb and the pockets — cause → correction

No maple pocketsMaple sugar dissolved into the batter. Fold it in LAST, ≤5 strokes, and griddle promptly — the longer it sits stirred-in, the more it melts. Keep the crystals discrete.
Gummy / scorched crumbLiquid syrup got into the batter, or the surface ran too hot. Syrup belongs only in the finishing butter (outside); hold the griddle at ~300°F / 150°C and cover so it cooks through low-and-slow.
Stack slides apartComponents mis-matched in size. Smash sausage to ~4″ (→ ~3.5″), fold the egg to a cake-sized pad, and stack with the maple-buttered faces inward.
