Method · The Bhuna

Build the Curry

Method stovetop — oil separation gates every stage

  1. Onions: 100g ghee, add onions. 50–60 min medium, stir every 5–7 min. Done: volume down ~75%, no steam releasing, smell sweet not sharp, color deep brown & jammy.
  2. Blend: tilt the pot, immersion-blend the onions to a smooth paste in the residual fat — no water. (Traditional blender: remove center cap, cover with towel, vent steam.)
  3. Fry the paste (bhuna): 30g ghee, return paste, 8–10 min medium, stir constantly. Done: paste looks curdled and matte, clear oil pools in "craters" and at the edges. Non-negotiable — rushing = raw taste.
  4. Bloom: push paste to the sides, add whole spices to the fat in the center, 30–45 sec until fragrant and sizzling, stir in. Add 60g ginger-garlic paste, 2–3 min until the raw smell is gone.
  5. Ground spices: medium-low. Add the ground spices + the first 3.5g garam masala (bloom half). Fry 45–60 sec in the fat. Splash 50 ml water, cook until oil separates.
  6. Yogurt: low heat. Temper first — whisk ~5g hot paste into the yogurt bowl (prevents curdling). Add ~30g at a time, waiting for oil to separate before each. 10–12 min total.
  7. Sear: high heat. Add chicken, season with ~12g salt (hold the rest). Sear 4–5 min until opaque — building fond.
  8. Simmer: add ~300 ml hot water, scrape up the fond, cover and simmer until the chicken is cooked through — ~15–20 min for thigh chunks. Stir once or twice.
  9. Reduce: uncover, medium-high. Reduce until the sauce clings to the chicken, 8–12 min. Now taste and bring to final salt (the held ~6g, to taste) — after reduction, not before.
  10. Garlic finish: small pan, 20g ghee + 30g minced garlic + slit chilies. 60–90 sec to just-golden edges (not brown). Pour into the curry.
  11. Finish: off heat. Add the second 3.5g garam masala (finish half), kasuri methi, 15g lemon. The garlic should hit your nose immediately.
The bhuna — oil separation is the whole game: it happens at steps 3, 5, and 6. The paste goes wet/glossy → matte/curdled → oil pools in craters. The oil should be tinted red/yellow — clear oil means the spices haven't released. Rush this and the curry is raw and flat.

Troubleshoot · Notes

Read & Adjust

Troubleshooting

ProblemCauseFix
Watery / thin sauceUnder-reduced onions; bhuna rushedReduce longer at step 9; oil must separate at steps 3, 5, 6
Tastes raw / flatBhuna rushed; bloom skippedNo shortcuts on oil separation; bloom the spices in fat
No garlic punchFinish garlic overcookedGolden edges only, not brown; add right before serving
Too hot / dryChili concentrated; not enough fatAdd 20–30g ghee — NOT water. Fat mellows heat.
Yogurt curdledCold yogurt; heat too highRoom temp; temper first; keep heat low

Notes & Scaling

Onion ratio: 1:1 minimum. If the sauce runs thin, go 1.25:1.

Fat: 10% is conservative — restaurants run 12–15%. Dry or too spicy? Add ghee, never water.

Yogurt: room temp, temper first, low heat — the three guards against curdling.

Whole spices: bloom in fat — extracts more than dry heat alone.

Make ahead: better the next day. Add fresh-fried garlic when reheating to bring the punch back.

Scaling: doubles linearly — but the onion fry stretches to 70–80 min at 2×.

Why no tomato: the body comes from the deeply browned onion paste and the yogurt, not acid + sugar from tomato. It's a richer, rounder base — the garlic finish and kasuri methi carry the top notes.
